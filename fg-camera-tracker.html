How to get a good track

  1. Mount the phone/webcam on a tripod — a steady, fixed camera is the single biggest factor in accuracy.
  2. Pick Side-on to measure speed/angle/apex/hang, or Behind to judge left/right and make / no good.
  3. Frame the shot so the ball's whole flight is visible and the background behind the arc is fairly plain (sky is ideal).
  4. Set the scale: click two points a known distance apart (uprights = 18.5 ft is a reliable one), then enter that distance.
  5. Hit ARM & TRACK, then kick. It records the moving ball, auto-stops when the ball leaves, and shows the metrics.
  6. If it tracks your leg/body instead of the ball, raise Sensitivity and the Ignore bottom %.
This is vision-based estimation, not radar. A single consumer camera can reasonably estimate launch angle, hang time, apex, rough ball speed, and (behind view) left/right and make/miss — but not spin rate, and accuracy depends on a steady camera, good lighting, and an honest scale reference. Real TrackMan uses Doppler radar hardware; this is a free, browser-only approximation built in the same spirit.
