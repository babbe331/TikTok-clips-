# The ROI of Faster Follow-Up (With the Actual Math)

"Respond faster" sounds like generic advice until you put real numbers on it.
So let's do that. Here's exactly what improving your speed-to-lead and follow-up
is worth to a real estate team — in dollars.

## Start with your lead economics

Take a team with typical numbers:

- **100 leads/month** from paid sources
- **~2% conversion** to a closed deal (so ~2 deals/month)
- **$9,000 average commission** per closed deal

That's about **$18,000/month** in commission from those leads, and you're
already paying for every one of them whether you convert it or not.

## Now apply a realistic improvement

Responding in minutes instead of hours, and following up 8–12 times instead of
1–2, doesn't require magic — it's well-documented to lift conversion. Say it
moves you from **2% to 3%**:

- 100 leads × 3% = **3 deals/month** (up from 2)
- That's **one extra deal every month**
- One extra deal × $9,000 = **$9,000/month** in new commission
- Annually: **~$108,000** from leads you were *already paying for*

You didn't buy more leads. You stopped wasting the ones you had.

## Compare that to the cost

A done-for-you automation system runs around $1,500–$5,000/month. Even at the
top of that range, against $9,000+ in new monthly commission, you're looking at
roughly a **2–6× return** — and that's from a single extra deal. Most teams that
fix follow-up gain more than one.

| | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leads/month | 100 | 100 |
| Conversion | 2% | 3% |
| Deals/month | 2 | 3 |
| Commission/month | $18,000 | $27,000 |
| **Extra/month** | — | **+$9,000** |

## The hidden second return: time

There's a second ROI that doesn't show up in the table: the hours your agents
get back. Qualifying, chasing, and onboarding by hand eats time they could spend
closing. Automating it doesn't just convert more leads — it frees your best
people to do the work only they can do.

## Run your own numbers

Plug in your real lead count, conversion rate, and average commission. The
honest version of this math — even with conservative assumptions — almost always
favors fixing follow-up. It's one of the rare investments in real estate where
the downside is small and the upside is a recurring extra deal.
